# Continuity State

## Current Story State

Day two of the storm. On the first night the five found the school impossibly maintained — a hand-kept library ledger dated eleven days ago, cut-back greenhouse vines, five of fifty dorm rooms made up for a letter that went to sixty. That morning they went down the service stairs off the library into the archive vaults and found post-graduation dossiers on every alumnus, current within days: relationship status, address, occupation, notable events, family ties — their own private lives filed by someone, each name carrying a percentage (Fells 88, Sarah 84, Marcus 81, David 79, Elena 73). Alex Voss's file is a twenty-year stack, addressed SITE, dated yesterday, marked 100% in fresh red ink. They have carried the folders upstairs and intend to find whoever keeps them. Nobody has said what the numbers might mean. On day two David ran a forty-one-row fault log of the building and found every reading either impossibly clean or unmeasurable; Elena reported a fifth ledger line (transfer complete, 23:59); a motionless figure stood in the white-out facing the school and moved only when unobserved, keeping impossible detail at distance (Sarah's observation — never called anything but what it looked like); and at ~2:03 a.m. something knocked three times, patiently, at metronome intervals on the walkway's outer door for eleven minutes, then cut off mid-rhythm. Nobody answered it. The five now sleep together in the common room around the dead fire. Jake says it was a person; Marcus said no. On day three Marcus set the crew to proving the exits: Jake and Elena walked the residential corridor both ways and found it measures two different distances — eleven minutes outbound toward the library stair, seven coming back — while photographs of the same corridor show only its normal length (the camera does not see the extra). David entered Jake's pencil tally into the fault log: same corridor, two distances, both true. Sarah concluded aloud that it isn't the hallway, it's us walking it. Jake noticed, and told no one, that his hands have been steady all day.
