# The Buffer Failure

## Setup

Five people who have not been in a room together in twenty years answer a reunion letter and drive into a blizzard. The school is pristine — too warm, too bright, too sharp. They remember almost nothing about it, and none of them can say why. Alex Voss, the Custodian, has been waiting. He mailed the letters himself. He is about to try again.

## Rising Action

The school begins to lose resolution. Phosphor-green glitches, hallways that run longer than they were, the pool showing them the town already melted. The friends find the dossiers — their own faces, their own lives, and a number printed beside each name. The siege repeats: roughly ten to twelve loops, each a re-boot, each slightly different, each harder to reset than the last. Alex grows more desperate with every cycle. Critical injuries accumulate; minor ones vanish. Only the digital record survives intact — which is why Elena's notes and Jake's photographs become the memory the group cannot hold in their heads.

## Climax

Around the second-to-last loop, something changes: they begin to remember the earlier loops. The knowledge does not make it easy — it makes it *possible*. They reach the Custodian's logs and understand what Alex is, and what he has been doing: every rescue has been an execution. In the final loop, Jake is deleted holding the record together, and Alex — for the first time in twenty years — opens the door and lets them go, knowing it takes him with it.

## Resolution

They get out. It has been four and a half years outside and only a season inside. The world re-stabilises behind them. **One ending only: no loop, no branching.** They walk out of a school that no longer exists, carrying the only surviving record of what happened — and the memory of the friend who kept it.
