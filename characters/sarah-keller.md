# Sarah Keller

## Appearance

Five-foot-ten and poised, with the easy stillness of someone who has been photographed for a living and learned to hold a pose without effort. Dark hair, high cheekbones, a face that the camera has always liked. She dresses in quiet, expensive neutrals. What people notice last, if at all, is that she almost never touches anything first.

## Personality & Traits

Quietly, constantly observant. Sarah watches more than she speaks and remembers rooms the way other people remember conversations — the exact color of the light, the way a doorway framed someone, whether a face looked sharp or wrong. She is warm with David in private and reserved everywhere else. Her stillness is not calm. It is attention.

**Strengths:** Reads a room instantly; uncanny eye for when something looks wrong; steady, patient presence
**Flaws:** Mistakes appearance for identity; withdraws instead of asking for help; will endure a distortion silently rather than admit she is losing her grip on the world

## Backstory

Sarah and David married young and moved to Oregon, where she built a career out of being seen — modeling, then brand work, then funding the work of people who made beautiful things. She was good at it, and it cost her something she could not name. She can describe the last twenty years in perfect visual detail and cannot remember a single thing she felt about any of it.

## Motivations & Goals

**External:** Stay recognizable — to David, to the group, to herself.
**Internal:** Discover that she is more than the thing that gets looked at.
**Fear:** Becoming low-resolution. Losing the edges of herself and not being able to tell when it happened.

## Location References

- the-archive-school — a place she has described in detail and never actually seen clearly in memory

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Understated and precise, with a habit of describing how things look instead of how they feel. She uses aesthetic vocabulary without irony — "flat," "sharp," "washed out" — as if texture were a kind of truth. When she is frightened she goes even quieter and stiller, which is the tell.

> "The light in here's gone flat. Has been for about an hour. Nobody else noticed."

> "I don't mind forgetting the years. I mind forgetting what I looked like in them."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Identifies with her own resolution — the image of herself is the self.
- **Key turning points:** The first time a reflection lags; David failing to find her face in a photo; the long loop where she navigates by remembering rooms instead of seeing them.
- **Ending state:** Discovers she is not the picture. Comes out of the school having lost some sharpness and gained something she could never model.
