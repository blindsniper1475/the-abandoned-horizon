# Marcus Hayes

## Appearance

Six-foot-two and broad-shouldered, with the weathered look of a man who has spent fifteen years outdoors in a hard country. Short-cropped hair going gray at the temples, a close beard he trims without vanity, and hands thick with callus. A pale scar crosses his left shoulder, older than the marriage — a horse accident, early on the trail, the kind of injury the job is supposed to produce. He carries himself like a door that has been closed for years — still, heavy, hard to move.

## Personality & Traits

Stoic and steady to the point of seeming cold. He speaks slowly and means every word; there is no small talk in him. He is the group's anchor — the one who checks the locks, counts the heads, takes the last watch. The weight in his eyes is not fear but inventory: always counting what he still has, and what it will cost to keep it.

**Strengths:** Unshakeable under pressure; decisive in a crisis; physically capable; calms a room by refusing to panic
**Flaws:** Cannot admit fear or weakness; mistakes control for care; reaches for force before patience

## Backstory

Marcus and Elena married the summer after graduation and left Alaska for Texas, and that was the whole story. He went straight to work practically out of high school — a Texas Ranger, first on small posts, then on the trail crews and detail work, then the department's training cadre — and built a life measured in procedures and held lines. It was never glamour work. It was shifts, reports, and being the one who went in first. Fifteen years in, they have a son, Chris, thirteen. What he has never been able to explain — to Elena, or to himself — is why the years before Texas will not sit still in his memory. He remembers the shape of the school, the smell of the halls, a handful of faces. Everything else arrives out of order, like a photograph cut into strips and shuffled.

## Motivations & Goals

**External:** Keep the five of them alive and get them out.
**Internal:** Forgive himself for leaving the first time — for walking out of the school and letting it erase him.
**Fear:** That when it matters most he will freeze; that the line breaks and it is his fault, exactly as it already was once.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Economical and low. Short declaratives. He gives orders as statements, never requests, and rarely asks a question he does not already know the answer to. Under stress his sentences get shorter and quieter — he never shouts. When he is truly afraid, he goes still.

> "Nobody moves until I say the hall is clear."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Believes safety is something you hold by force of will, and that his job is to stand between the others and the dark.
- **Key turning points:** The first loop that resets him anyway; the dossier with a number printed over his own name; the discovery that his blank years are not grief but deletion.
- **Ending state:** Lets go of the line. The escape only becomes possible when he stops trying to hold the room and starts leading the way out of it.
