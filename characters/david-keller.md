# David Keller

## Appearance

Five-foot-nine and lean, with the slight forward stoop of a man who has spent two decades bent toward screens. Restless hands — a pen spun, a thumb tapped, a ring turned. Sharply tired eyes behind glasses he pushes up out of habit. He dresses in the same gray and charcoal he has worn since college, functionally, without any interest in the question of clothes.

## Personality & Traits

Quick, exacting, and constitutionally unable to let a problem alone. David thinks in systems and speaks in diagnostics: he does not ask how someone feels, he asks what changed. He is dryly funny and genuinely kind, but he processes the world as a machine with something wrong with it, and he is happiest when he has a fault to chase.

**Strengths:** Rigorous, inventive under constraint, the only one who can read the school's shivering infrastructure
**Flaws:** Needs everything to have an explanation; mistakes understanding for mastery; cannot sit with a problem he is not allowed to solve

## Backstory

David married Sarah not long after graduation and moved to Oregon, where he worked as a systems and security analyst for a company he described to his friends as "a firewall with a payroll." He was good, and he knew it. The one thing that never fit his model was his own memory of high school — a gap with no error log, no cause, no fix. He has read about it, theorized about it, and told no one.

## Motivations & Goals

**External:** Find the way out — the back door, the patch, the off switch.
**Internal:** Accept that being useful is not the same as being in control.
**Fear:** Finding the exit and being wrong about it. Running the same calculation twice and getting two answers.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Fast, precise, faintly impatient. He explains things in systems language whether or not anyone asked. He interrupts himself to correct himself. When he is rattled, he starts narrating probabilities out loud — a habit the others used to find funny and now find frightening.

> "That's not a coincidence, that's a recurrence. There's a difference and it matters."

> "I've solved every puzzle I've ever been handed. I've never once been handed myself."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Certain that any broken thing yields to enough analysis, including the school and including him.
- **Key turning points:** The geometry that refuses his math; his logic producing two answers; the realization that the exit was never a secret to be found.
- **Ending state:** The one who pays for the lesson — he learns the truth about the back door at the exact moment he can no longer use it. A change-negative arc: the analyst who analysed himself into the wall.
