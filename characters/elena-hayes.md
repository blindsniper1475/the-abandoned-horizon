# Elena Hayes

## Appearance

Average height and quick in the shoulders, with dark hair usually pinned back and out of the way. Sharp, restless eyes that land on details other people walk past. She dresses for movement — practical layers, flat boots — and there is always a pen somewhere on her, even now. Her hands are never quite still unless she is writing.

## Personality & Traits

Meticulous and relentlessly curious. Elena notices the misplaced chair, the second set of footprints, the light that flickers on a rhythm. She is warm with the people she loves and clinical with everything else. When facts stop adding up she does not panic — she gets quiet and starts writing, which is how she handles fear.

**Strengths:** Observant, methodical, calm under information overload, the group's institutional memory
**Flaws:** Confuses documentation with control; keeps notes instead of confiding; will chase a question past the point of safety

## Backstory

Elena married Marcus the summer after graduation and went with him to Texas. She built a career as an online reporter, known for long, careful pieces about local histories that everyone else had given up on — old schools, closed factories, families who vanished from the record. She never told Marcus why those stories, in particular. She spent twenty years investigating her own hometown without ever being able to say, out loud, that she could not remember it.

## Motivations & Goals

**External:** Find out what happened to them before they were written out — and get it down in writing before it is lost again.
**Internal:** Accept that a record is a lantern, not a wall.
**Fear:** That the notes she is so proud of will one day be in her own handwriting, and she will not remember writing them.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Precise and probing. She asks more questions than she answers and presses on the vague ones. She quotes dates and figures from memory without thinking about it. When she is frightened she narrates — out loud, in the flat register of a reporter filing copy.

> "That light's flickered four times, same interval. Write that down."

> "I have three months of memories and twenty years of evidence that they're wrong."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Believes the truth, properly documented, is a thing she can stand on.
- **Key turning points:** Her own handwriting in the logs she cannot remember writing; the dossier that names her as a subject; the loop where her notes save them and her memory does not.
- **Ending state:** Learns that her record is the group's real memory — and that the point of writing it down was never control. It was so someone could find the way back.
