# Alex Voss

## Appearance

Not clearly seen. Alex is described by the others only in fragments, and the fragments disagree — a tall figure in a maintenance coat, a silhouette at the end of a corridor that is not there when the light moves, a voice with a texture like a hallway heard through a wall. He carries a ring of keys he never sets down. When he is almost visible, he looks like a man who has not slept or eaten or aged right in a very long time.

## Personality & Traits

Patient, kind, and entirely unreasonable. Alex is not cruel — he is *devoted*, which is worse. He speaks to the five like a man coaxing a family out of a burning house that he himself keeps relighting, and he means every reassurance he gives them. He has no interest in power and no awareness that he is the disaster. He has done the same thing so many times that he can no longer tell the difference between trying and repeating.

**Strengths:** Retention 100% — the only person the Void cannot take; genuine authority over the school's systems; inexhaustible
**Flaws:** Cannot stop; cannot see what he has become; measures success in how long they last rather than whether they leave

## Backstory

Alex was the Custodian. When the school was decommissioned and its people scattered, he did not scatter. He stayed to keep the facilities running, to file the records, to hold the place open against the weather and the dark. Nobody told him the town was on a list of things the world had decided to forget. By the time he understood, he had become the only thing still holding the door — and the door had begun to hold him.

He mailed the reunion letter himself. On good days he believed it was to gather them for a twentieth anniversary. On the days that matter, he knows he called them back because he cannot reach them any other way.

## Motivations & Goals

**External:** Re-boot the school, again, and bring them through it safely this time.
**Internal:** Be forgiven. Be told it was not his fault. Be let go.
**Fear:** That there is no version of this where he walks out too — and that he has spent years proving it, one loop at a time.

## Voice & Speech Patterns

Warm, low, unhurried — the voice of a man reading a bedtime story that he has read too many times. He uses the plural "we" and the future tense constantly: *we'll do this again, we'll get it right, next time.* He never says goodbye and never finishes a sentence in which he leaves. As the loops accumulate, the warmth does not fade; the *certainty* sharpens, and that is what makes it frightening.

> "You're doing so well. Better than last time. Sit down, let me get the lights."

> "I've had a lot of practice at this. That's not a bad thing. That's the only good thing."

## Character Arc

- **Starting state:** Certain he can fix it. The Custodian with a plan and a set of keys.
- **Key turning points:** The first loop that fails anyway; the loop where he stops explaining; the last loop, in which he finally understands that every rescue has been an execution.
- **Ending state:** Change-negative. Alex does not get better — he gets *clearer*. On the final loop he does the one thing he has never done: he opens the door and lets them go, knowing it takes him with it.
