# The Archive School

## Description

A brutalist campus fused into a single organism. Twelve concrete towers rise ten to twelve floors out of the fog, packed so tightly into four acres that from a distance they read as one structure — a gray fist at the edge of the sea. There are no gaps to the weather: enclosed corridors and covered walkways stitch the buildings together at every third floor, and the sub-basement runs beneath all of it, linking every tower below ground.

The **library** is its own enormous building, the tallest and quietest on campus. The **greenhouse** is *vertical* — floor after floor of glass and growing light, stacked like a cabinet of weather. The **academic blocks** hold classrooms; the **dormitories** hold beds; the **service blocks** hold kitchens, staff rooms, and the long low halls where the town used to eat together.

Outside: the White-Out. Inside: warmth, floor wax, and old paper.

## History

Built as a school that could also hold a town. Because the storms never truly stop on that coast, the campus was designed so that a whole population could live, eat, sleep, and shelter without stepping outside — the town's designated refuge for the worst of the weather. It worked. Then the weather got worse than weather, and the world quietly stopped counting the place.

## Culture & Customs

- The **sub-basement shelters** were communal: families bedded down in the dormitory halls, the kitchen ran all night, and nobody slept alone.
- **Reading the corridors** — locals learned to navigate the interlinked buildings by counting doorframes, because the covered walkways all look identical in a storm.
- The school ran a **twenty-year reunion** tradition. Only one was ever held.

## Notable Features

- **The Library** — separate, enormous, almost the size of the rest of the campus.
- **The Vertical Greenhouse** — stacked floors of glass; the Rot is strongest there.
- **The Sub-Basement** — full four-acre footprint; dorms, shelter, and the deep files.
- **The Pool** — indoor, now a data-sink where the decay can be *seen*.
- **The Clock Tower** — where the Custodian's logs are kept.

## Current State

Pristine on arrival and rotting by the inch. In Act I the school is over-warm, over-lit, and too sharp to be real — nostalgia with the resolution turned up. As the loops accumulate, hallways run longer than they were, colors bleed phosphor-green, and the building starts to *breathe*. By the end it is not a school. It is a threshold, and it is closing.
