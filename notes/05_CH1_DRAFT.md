# 05 — CHAPTER 1 DRAFT

**Title:** The Archive of Ghosts
**Status:** First draft (first pass)
**Goal:** Establish the reunion, the warmth of the group, and the too-perfect "pristine" surface of the school.

---

The blizzard was a wall of white-out, a physical weight that pressed against the windows of the transport truck. It erased the horizon and turned the world into a sea of static. Inside, the air smelled of stale coffee, old leather, and the sharp, metallic scent of the heater struggling against the Alaskan cold.

Marcus sat in the front, his hands—calloused and steady—gripping the armrest. He looked at the others, his face etched with the weary authority of a man who had spent years holding the line. Beside him, Elena watched the fog-blurred world outside. She had her notebook in her lap, her eyes sharp, looking for the story in the silence.

"Twenty years," Elena whispered, her voice barely carrying over the hum of the heater. "It feels like we were just in the hallways. Just yesterday."

"Time is a funny thing," David muttered from the back. He was staring at a small tablet, his brow furrowed. "It feels like we're being squeezed into a smaller space. The further north we get, the tighter it feels."

Sarah and Jake were huddled together. Sarah watched the way the light hit the frost on the glass, her face perfectly poised even in the middle of a storm. She looked like a painting—still and observant. Jake had his camera up, catching the way the fog swallowed the trees.

"It's the vibe," Jake said, his voice relaxed, almost airy. "The school. It's like a big, old memory. It's waiting for us."

Then, the school appeared.

It didn't just emerge; it loomed. It stood like a fortress of concrete and steel, rising out of the white void. The concrete was clean, the windows dark and reflective, and the architecture was heavy—a building designed to keep things *in* as much as to keep things *out*.

As they pulled into the courtyard, the white-out seemed to retreat, bowing before the school.

"It looks... perfect," Sarah whispered. "Like a photograph."

They stepped out into the biting cold. The air was so still it felt heavy. They gathered in the courtyard—a group of friends who had lived separate lives, marriages and careers and the years of a world they'd left behind—now standing in the center of a school that had been waiting for them.

They entered the main hall. The interior was warm, the air smelling of floor wax and old paper. The lights were warm, the hallways long and straight, and the whole place was heavy with the weight of the past. Everything was sharp. The colors were vivid. It was the shelter of their youth.

They sat in the common room, the fire crackling. They talked about their lives, their families, the years that had passed. They laughed, and for a moment, the school was just a school.

"It's like we never left," Marcus said, his voice deep and steady. He looked around at the others—his family.

They sat in the warmth, the firelight dancing on the walls. They felt safe. Everything was clear and certain.

But as the night deepened, a flicker occurred.

A light at the far end of the hallway stuttered. Not a power surge—a stutter of *something.* A flash of phosphor-green, so fast it was almost a trick of the eye.

Elena noticed it. She leaned forward, eyes narrowing. "Did you see that?"

"Just the old wiring," Marcus said, though his voice had an edge of caution. "It's an old building. It's settling."

They laughed it off. They settled into the chairs, the firelight on the walls. The surface held. They were safe.

But somewhere in the shadows of the hallway, the storm seemed to hum a different note.
