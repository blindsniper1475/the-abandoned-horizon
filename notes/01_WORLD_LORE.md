# 01 — WORLD LORE (DIGITAL DECAY)

*Internal reference only. These terms are never spoken in the narrative prose.*

---

## THE VOID
A state of "De-Rez": reality loses its resolution and becomes **"Low-Bit."**
The Void is not evil so much as **indifferent**. It is where things that are no longer wanted go.

## THE ROT
A corruption of reality. It manifests as:
- **Phosphor-green glitches** (#00FF41)
- **Non-Euclidean geometry**
- **"Data-Rot"** — texture, memory, and meaning decaying together

## THE ARCHIVE
A metaphysical layer where the world **"discards"** what it no longer wants.
People, places, and moments that reality has forgotten are stored here.

## ABSCISSA INDUSTRIES
A corporate-occult conglomerate that treats **human consciousness as data to be optimized.**
- **Synthesis:** the creation of protected, "pristine" realities.
- **Archives:** the storage of realities that have been discarded.

## MEMORY & THE PLACE
The school leaves a mark on the memory of everyone who passes through it.
- **Those who leave forget.** Anyone who walks out of the school—out of the town—**automatically forgets** what happened there. It is a property of the place, not the person. Distance dissolves it.
- **Suppression compounds it.** On top of the place's effect, the friends each buried things themselves—doorways in the mind they chose to close.
- **What is left is fragmentary.** Not true amnesia, but **snippets**: a face, a hallway, a voice—**jumbled and out of order**, like a shuffled deck. The pieces survive; the sequence does not.

> The horror implication: the memories are still in there. Coming back to the place may bring them up—but broken, displaced, and wrong.

## THE LETTER (THE REUNION INVITATION)
- **Alex** mailed the reunion letter—a **20th-anniversary invitation**—to the entire graduating class.
- Everyone received one. **Only five came.**

## RETENTION
Every person processed by the system is scored on **RETENTION**—their **compatibility with the Void / the Noise**, given as a percentage.
- The **higher** the number, the longer a person *holds* before the Void takes them.
- **Alex scored 100%**—built for the Noise, bound to the place.
- **The five sit between 73% and 88%.** High, but not the highest.
- Full roster and subject dossiers: `08_ARCHIVE_RECORDS.md`.

## THE NOISE
The **Noise** is the Void's signal—the static of it. What the friends hear as the **"White-Out" blizzard** is the Noise pressing against the school.
- The school's job is to *stay loud enough to drown it out.*
- The Echoes are what the Noise leaves behind when it gets in.

## THE BUFFER
A spiritual/digital barrier that holds reality together—the "skin" of a place.
- When it holds: the world is **sharp and vivid**.
- When it fails: the world **melts**.

## DE-REZ
The process of the Buffer failing. Reality degrades into a liminal, **low-resolution** state.

---

## THE ECHOES — TAXONOMY
Entities born from the Rot. They are the "discarded" pieces of reality pressing in from outside.

### 1. Static Echoes
Frozen in a single moment, repeating one action forever.
- A student forever trying to open a door that isn't there anymore.
- A teacher caught mid-sentence, looping.
- Harmless alone—but relentless, and endless in number. The background noise of the Archive.

### 2. Corrupted Echoes
Aggressive, distorted versions of discarded people.
- They are the ones who noticed they were being forgotten, and fought it.
- They hunt. They remember what they were—badly.

### 3. Buffer-Breakers
Large, non-Euclidean entities that physically warp the geometry around them.
- They don't hunt so much as *erode*.
- Where they pass, hallways stretch and rooms fold.

---

## THE ALEX PARADOX
Alex was the **Custodian** of the school. He is caught **"in-between"** dimensions.
He is trying to **"re-boot"** the school to save the friends.

### THE COST OF A RE-BOOT
Every re-boot makes the school **more corrupted**.
- Each re-boot pushes the Buffer down another notch.
- The distortions become **more visible each time**—geometry slips further, colors bleed, more of the Rot shows through.
- Alex is not fixing anything. He is **hastening the collapse**—whether he knows it or not.

> This is the tragic engine of the story: the Custodian's attempts to save them are the very thing killing the school.

### ALEX'S DESCENT
**Every loop makes Alex more desperate.** He is not getting closer—he is getting worse. With each re-boot, the man on the other side of the wall sounds less like a custodian and more like something that has been trying too long.

---

## THE LOOPS (THE RE-BOOT CYCLES)
The friends don't live the siege once. They live it **again and again**—each loop a re-boot, each re-boot a slightly different version of the same nightmare.
- **Roughly 10–12 loops** in total.
- **Each loop is a little different**—the corruption has grown, so the school is never the same twice.
- **Resetting gets harder every time.** The school resists. Each reset costs more, takes longer, and leaves more damage behind.
- **Near the end—around the second-to-last loop—it gets *easier*.** By then they have begun to **remember the earlier loops**, and that broken knowledge is an edge.
- **The final loop ends the only way it ever could: they get out.**

> This is where **memory becomes a mechanic**: remembering across the loops is what finally lets them escape.

### WHAT CARRIES ACROSS A LOOP
Not everything resets.
- **Anything digital — fully.** Files, records, screens, and **Jake's photographs** survive every reset intact. Digital memory is the one thing the school can't scrub.
- **Key items — physically.** **Key cards** (access to certain buildings), **keys**, **photographs**, and **notes** come through with them.
- **Memories — only partially.** They keep fragments. Some loops blur away; others stick hard. Never the whole.
- **Injuries — unevenly.** **Critical injuries carry over, but are *less severe* each loop**—they heal a little every reset but never fully vanish. **Minor damage disappears completely** with each loop. By the end, they carry the accumulated scar of every loop.

> Because the *digital* record outlives the *human* one, the friends can leave themselves a trail—**Elena's notes and Jake's photos are memories they can't hold in their own heads.** That trail is how the escape finally becomes possible.

## THE TIME INSIDE
Time does not run the same way inside the school.
- **It feels like three months.** That is all they remember—three months of going through it.
- **Outside, four and a half years (4.5) pass** before they are completely free.
- The loops stretch real time thin while compressing what they experience of it.

> They walk out into a world that has aged **4.5 years** while they have only lived a season of it.

## THE TICKING CLOCK
Over 24 chapters the Buffer drops from **100% → 0%**. The school physically melts as the blizzard and the Echoes press in.
At the bottom, they **get out**—and the world **re-stabilizes**. The *book* does **not** end on a repeating cycle—but the *story* does contain loops along the way (see **THE LOOPS**).

### Buffer → Symptom progression (LOCKED)
The decaying world is only half of it. **The body fails on the same curve.** It begins like a sickness—something you'd wave off—and ends in forgetting who you are.

**The four stages:** **The Chill → The Drift → The Fracture → The Forgetting.**

| Buffer | The World | The Body (the duress) |
|---|---|---|
| **100%** | Pristine. Sharp. Vivid. Nostalgic. | Nothing. A warmth, an ease—almost *too* calm. |
| **~85%** | Faint wrongnesses. A flicker at the edge of vision. | **The Chill begins.** A dull headache. Ears ring. A heaviness behind the eyes. Dismissed as travel and fatigue. |
| **75%** | Light stutters. A shadow that lingers wrong. | **Flu-like.** Chills, nausea, aching joints, gray skin, a bone-tired heaviness. Everyone feels like they're *coming down with something.* |
| **60%** | Geometry begins to slip. Doors sit a little off. | **The Drift.** Small gaps in time. Words arrive wrong. Trouble following a conversation. |
| **50%** | The Echoes get in. Hallways run longer than they were. | **Disorientation.** Dizziness. Recent and old events start to blur together. Notes that don't match memory. |
| **35%** | Rooms fold. Sound drops in and out. | **The Fracture.** Can't hold onto what happened an hour ago. Names go first, then faces. |
| **25%** | Physical distortion. The halls lengthen; the building seems to breathe. | **Deep fracture.** Past and present bleed. The people beside you feel like strangers. |
| **10%** | Reality collapse. The Void floods in. | **The Forgetting.** They lose themselves—who they are, why they came, who the others are. |
| **0%** | The bottom—the school is consumed. | The Forgetting at its worst. **Then the escape.** |

> **Prose rule:** never state a percentage. The number is for *us*. In the text, the drop is felt as **feeling ill, then confused, then lost.**

> **THE ENDING (LOCKED): One ending only.** They **escape**, and the world **re-stabilizes**. The *book* doesn't loop (no "cycle restarts," no "remain discarded")—but the *story* contains loops (see **THE LOOPS**).
